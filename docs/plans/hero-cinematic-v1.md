## 0. The idea in one paragraph

Today the hero is a clip cycle with a headline that assembles over it. The cinematic version is a
short film the visitor drives with the scroll wheel, the way Apple product pages work: a 5 to 10 s
drone-style shot of a RoboRacer car overtaking another on the ICRA 2026 track, generated by
Higgsfield from our own ICRA 2026 photos, split into frames and scrubbed on a canvas as the page
scrolls. Slow motion is intrinsic: 120 to 150 frames spread over three viewport heights of scroll.
The three headline lines land on beats of the footage (lead car fills the frame, the overtake,
both cars settle). An idle visitor still sees the film: after two seconds at the top the page
glides to the first beat on its own, once, as the current hero already does. Under reduced motion
or on a weak device: the locked frame as a poster, then the static headline on paper, exactly the
current fallback.

## 6. Post pipeline (`scripts/frames.sh`)

From the chosen take (`_harvest/higgsfield/<job>/result.mp4`, 16:9, 720p or 1080p):

1. `scripts/frames.sh probe` records fps, duration, size.
2. `scripts/frames.sh frames <mp4> public/media/hero-cine` extracts the scrub set:
   desktop `d/f_0001.webp ...` at 1600 wide or the source width, whichever is smaller (never
   upscaled; a 720p take gives 1280-wide frames, say so in the report), q 74; mobile
   `m/f_0001.webp ...` at 960 wide, q 72; caps of 160 desktop and 80 mobile frames (the script
   resamples the frame rate when the source has more); `poster-<width>.webp` for each set from
   frame 1; and `manifest.json` (`{count, width, height, fps_extracted, quality, bytes}` per set).
   Budgets: desktop set under 9 MB, mobile set under 2.5 MB; the script lowers quality in steps
   of 4, then the width by 10 percent, then the frame count, until it fits.
3. `scripts/frames.sh slowmo <mp4> <out>` makes the 0.5x minterpolated 1080p H.264 encode, no
   audio, for the Highlights strip or a later use; not needed by the hero itself.
4. `scripts/frames.sh report public/media/hero-cine` prints sizes for the report.

`.gitignore` gets `public/media/hero-cine/**` with `!public/media/hero-cine/manifest.json` and the
two posters re-included (posters under 150 KB each).

## 7. The component: `src/components/ui/HeroCinematic.tsx`

New file next to `HeroChapter.tsx`, which stays untouched. Reuse from it: the display type
constant, the headline markup (line 1 as one unit in the logo gradient, later lines word by word),
the pause control pattern is not needed (nothing plays), the scroll cue, the nav-fill contract
(`data-hero-chapter`, `data-nav-fill="from to"`), the static fallback; copy `isWeakDevice` and

`src/lib/frameSequence.ts`.
Frame engine: `src/lib/frameSequence.ts` (loader plus canvas drawer), reference implementation and
rules in `.claude/skills/hero-cinematic/references/frame-scrubber.md`. Adapt it; do not paste it
blind.

Schedule in pin progress p (chapter 400vh, sticky viewport, one scrubbed GSAP timeline as today):

| p | what |
|---|---|
| 0.00 to 0.05 | frame 1 (the locked composition), nav transparent, the film has not started |
| 0.05 to 0.80 | frames 1 to N, linear in p, `scrub: 0.8` (`SCRUB_START`, `SCRUB_END`) |
| beats A, B, C | the three headline lines land at `BEATS = [pA, pB, pC]`, each assembling over 0.10 of p (yPercent 110 to 0 plus opacity, ease in-out quart, as today); the director sets pA, pB, pC by reading the take's contact sheet: A when the lead car first fills its third of the frame, B at the overtake, C when both cars settle; defaults 0.30, 0.52, 0.70 |
| pA-0.06 to pC | block scale 1 to 1.30 (1.12 narrow), power1.inOut |
| pA-0.05 to pC | canvas brightness 1 to 0.42, saturate 1 to 0.8 (CSS filter on the canvas layer), so the first words land on footage that is still readable as footage |
| pC+0.02 | description fades in, no movement |
| 0.80 to 1.00 | hold on the last frame, full text, no transforms |
| 0.80 to 0.97 | nav fill `data-nav-fill="0.80 0.97"` |
| scroll-out q | unchanged from `HeroChapter`: words fade as one 0.22 to 0.62 (power2.inOut), canvas brightness 0.42 to 0.12 over 0 to 0.6, the Highlights strip slides over |

Idle glide: when the coarse frame set is loaded and the visitor is still at the top after
`GLIDE_DELAY_S = 2`, glide once to `GLIDE_TARGET_P` over a duration that keeps the film at 24 fps
or better (`duration = framesCovered / 24`, so about 4 s for 96 frames), cancel on any input,
never under reduced motion. Default `GLIDE_TARGET_P = 0.55` (past beat B).

Loading: the poster `<img>` (frame 1 as WebP, `fetchPriority="high"`) is the LCP element and
shows immediately with a 12 s push-in (scale 1 to 1.06) while frames load. The frame set loads
after `window` `load`: coarse pass first (every 6th frame, 6 fetches in flight), then the rest in
order; the canvas fades in over the poster (200 ms) once the coarse pass is complete; until then
scroll still runs the headline schedule over the poster, so nothing waits on the network. The
scrubber always draws the nearest loaded frame. Desktop set from 768px up, mobile set below;
`focusX` (0 to 1, default 0.5) sets the cover-fit anchor so the cars stay in frame on portrait
phones; the director tunes it from the mobile capture.

Canvas: sized to the viewport times `devicePixelRatio` capped at 2, cover-fit, redraw on resize,
draws coalesced to one per animation frame, `will-change` only while pinned (as today).

Reduced motion or weak device: poster at 100svh, then the static headline on paper (copy the
current fallback). No canvas, no frames fetched.

Caption: a mono tag bottom-left, `rendered from ICRA 2026 photographs`, `text-eyebrow`, on-ink
muted, visible from p 0.05; prop `caption` so Cedric can remove it in the morning. It is his call
whether the site labels the film; the default is honest.

Accessibility as today: one `h1` with the full sentence in `aria-label`, animated spans
`aria-hidden`, the canvas `aria-hidden` with the poster's alt on the `<img>`.
